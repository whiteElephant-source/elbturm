The tower sits on a Quasi-Island. The southern boundary of the property runs parallel to the Norderelbe at a distance of around 70 metres. To the south, its access to the river bank is cut off by a street. The north is delimited by the Oberhafenkanal. The site is located in the flood zone of Hamburg and therefore required to be raised from the river level by 8 metres. East of the site runs a motorway leading from Hamburg‘s city centre to the major access onto the A7 and to the west it is cut off by train tracks and another motorway. 

Just opposite the Elbtower, at the other bank of the Elbe River, lies Entenwerderpark. To us it presents itself as a real- life version of Arnold Böcklin’s Toteninsel. In his painting, cypresses are evocative of death and mourning. Here, poplars tremble in the wind. As in the inner eye, the image of the Elbtower juxtaposes with that of the park island, what can be discerned of nature in the perfect arrangement of the poplars is violated by human hubris. On a hewn burial chamber in the rock on the Toteninsel, Böcklin added his initials and the inscription «non omnis moriar» (I will not die completely) from the third version onwards.
