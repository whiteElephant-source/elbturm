Currently in Hamburg 6.6 percent of office space is vacant. Almost two percent more than the German average. Still the value of these empty buildings remains quite high. As the potential rent of a real estate project defines its value – even an empty building with a potentially high yield rates the value of a portfolio high. Vacancy resulting in high portfolio values allows investors to continuously get more debt deals for future projects. They remain credit worthy and the land banking of the empty, unused properties will most likely result in higher resell prices down the road. The gap between the real market and the financial market becomes evident. Instead of decreasing rent and possibly elevating the buildings‘ use value – the theoretical exchange value is worth a lot more. This advantage of unused buildings or empty plots results in the practice of landbanking.
